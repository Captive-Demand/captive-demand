# Landing Page Brief — Free Direct Booking Site Design

**For:** the Claude Code project building this page
**From:** Jordan / Captive Demand
**Date:** 2026-09-14
**One job:** turn cold Meta ad traffic into a booked call.

---

## Read this first

This page has exactly one conversion goal: a visitor books a short call. Nothing else. No nav, no secondary CTAs, no "learn more about our services," no email capture as a fallback. Every section either moves someone toward booking the call or removes a reason not to.

The visitor is cold. They tapped a Meta ad about Airbnb commission fees while scrolling on their phone. They have never heard of Captive Demand. They are a small business owner who is busy, budget-conscious, and has been burned by agencies or is at least wary of them. They will give this page about five seconds before deciding whether to keep reading.

The page has to do three things, in this order:

1. **Prove it's worth their time** — the payoff is concrete and specific to their situation.
2. **Make the ask feel small** — the call is short, there's no pitch, and they get something real whether or not they ever pay us.
3. **Make booking frictionless** — the calendar is right there, on mobile, with minimal fields.

## Context

### Who we are
Captive Demand is a Nashville performance marketing and web agency. We run paid ads, build custom websites, and do SEO. We've run ads for unique-stay operators before and we build sites for hospitality clients. This page should feel like it comes from people who understand glamping and STR operators, not from a generic agency.

### The campaign this page serves
We're running Meta ads to owners of unique stays: glamping sites, domes, cabins, tiny-home resorts, small multi-unit STR properties. The ad that's working leads with Airbnb's commission. Ad copy currently live:

> **FOR MULTI-UNIT, UNIQUE STAYS**
> **Airbnb's cut is 15.5% now.**
> Fill your glamping rentals with guests you can rebook, commission-free.

That hook is the whole reason people click. The page must open by honoring it.

**Important:** the live ad's button currently says "Get My Free Ads" because it was built for a previous offer. The ad creative will be updated to match this new offer. Whatever the final ad headline and CTA say, the page headline and first CTA must echo them nearly word for word. Message match between ad and page is the single biggest lever on cold-traffic conversion. Confirm the final ad copy with Jordan before finalizing the hero.

### The offer
1. **We design a direct booking website for you, free.** A real design, specific to their property, not a template preview. The design is theirs to keep regardless of what happens next.
2. **To qualify, they book a short call with Jordan.** The call is a discovery conversation: what they care about, what booking platform they use (if any), whether they have a site, how many units, roughly how much they book through Airbnb. **The call is explicitly not a sales pitch.** We're promising that, and it has to be true.
3. **The design is delivered on a second call about a week later.** On that call, if they want us to build the site as a functioning direct booking system and run it for them, we'll tell them what that costs. That's a monthly managed service. **Do not put a price on this page** — the rate isn't finalized, and the page's job is to sell the call, not the build.

### Why this audience cares
Airbnb's host-side fee is real money. An operator doing $50,000 a year through Airbnb is paying somewhere around $7,750 in fees. A direct booking site lets them rebook past guests and capture some referral/search traffic without paying that cut. Most of them know this. What they don't have is a decent website, the time to build one, or any idea how to connect it to their calendar without double-booking.

What we know about them from the leads so far: their existing websites are almost uniformly bad. Many are probably Airbnb-only with no separate booking system. They're price-sensitive. They're not stupid, and they'll smell a bait-and-switch instantly.

**The 15.5% figure is verified.** Jordan is an Airbnb host and has confirmed it against his own account. Use it confidently.

## Page structure and copy direction

Single page. Sections in this order. Draft copy is provided as direction — refine it, but keep the voice: direct, plain, operator-to-operator. No agency jargon. No "leverage," "solutions," "elevate," "unlock." Short sentences. Talk like someone who's actually looked at their site.

### 1. Hero (above the fold on mobile — this is the whole ballgame)

**Eyebrow:** For multi-unit unique stays — glamping, domes, cabins, tiny homes

**Headline direction:** tie the commission pain to the free design in one line. Candidates:
- "Airbnb takes 15.5%. Your own booking site takes nothing."
- "Stop paying 15.5% to rebook guests who already love your place."
- "We'll design your direct booking site. Free. It's yours to keep."

Pick one that matches the final ad. The first two carry the hook; the third carries the offer. Best hero probably uses one as headline and the other as subhead.

**Subhead direction:** the offer in one sentence, then the ask in one sentence.
> We design a direct booking website for your property, free. All it takes is a 15-minute call so we know what you actually need.

**Primary CTA button:** "Book my 15-minute call" — or whatever the final ad CTA is. Orange/rust to match the ad. Large. Thumb-reachable on mobile.

**Trust line directly under the button (small text, do not skip this):**
> No sales pitch on the call. The design is yours whether you work with us or not.

**Visual:** match the ad. The ad uses a dark charcoal background with a glamping-dome landscape image at dusk. Use the same image family or the actual ad image. The visitor should feel like they landed in the same place they came from.

### 2. How it works — three steps, scannable

Make this a horizontal three-step strip on desktop, stacked on mobile. Icons optional, keep them simple.

**1. Book a 15-minute call**
Pick a time that works. Tell us about your property, how you take bookings now, and what's frustrating you.

**2. We design your booking site**
About a week later, we walk you through a design built for your property — your photos, your units, your booking flow.

**3. It's yours**
Keep the design. Build it yourself, hire whoever you want, or ask us to build and run it. No obligation either way.

### 3. What's in it for you — make the math concrete

This is the "worth their time" section. Make it specific, not aspirational.

**Heading direction:** "What 15.5% actually costs you"

**Body direction:** a short, concrete comparison. Something like:

> If you do $50,000 a year through Airbnb, you're paying around $7,750 in fees. Every guest you rebook directly is a guest you don't pay that on. Most operators we talk to could move 20–30% of their bookings direct within a year if they had a site worth sending people to. That's not a small number.

**Optional but recommended: a tiny calculator.** One slider or input — "Annual bookings through Airbnb" — that outputs "Estimated annual fees" at 15.5%. Nothing fancy. It makes the number theirs instead of ours, and it's the kind of thing people screenshot. Keep it to one input, one output. If it complicates the build meaningfully, skip it.

Caveat on the 20–30% claim: that's a directional estimate, not a measured stat. Either soften it ("many operators find they can move a meaningful share of bookings direct") or drop it. Don't invent a statistic.

### 4. What we'll cover on the call — kills the "what do they want from me" anxiety

**Heading direction:** "What the call is actually for"

**Body direction:** be transparent. List the four or five things we need to know, framed as "so the design fits."

> We're not going to pitch you. We're going to ask:
> - Where you manage your calendar and bookings today (Airbnb only? Vrbo? A booking system?)
> - Whether you have a website, and if so, what's wrong with it
> - How many units you have and what makes them different
> - What a guest goes through today if they want to book direct
> - What you'd want the site to do that it doesn't do now
>
> That's it. Fifteen minutes, maybe twenty if you're chatty. We use it to design something that fits your property instead of a generic template.

### 5. Why us — short, specific, no chest-thumping

Two or three sentences max. What matters to this audience: we know unique stays, we build sites, we understand booking platforms.

**Direction:**
> We're a Nashville agency that builds websites and runs ads for hospitality businesses, including unique stays. We know the booking platforms — which ones sync with Airbnb and Vrbo without double-booking, which ones let guests book without leaving your site. We've looked at a lot of glamping websites. Most of them are losing bookings they don't know about.

**Social proof placeholder:** if Jordan has a relevant client logo, a short quote from a unique-stay client, or a before/after of a site, it goes here. If not, omit the section rather than fake it. Do not use stock testimonials.

### 6. What's the catch — say it before they ask

This section does more for conversion than it looks like it should. The audience is skeptical, and naming the catch honestly builds more trust than pretending there isn't one.

**Heading direction:** "So what's the catch?"

**Body direction:**
> There isn't one, but here's the honest version. We design the site free because some people who see the design want us to build it and run it for them. That's a paid monthly service, and we'll tell you exactly what it costs on the second call — not before, and not by surprise. If you'd rather take the design somewhere else, that's fine. You'll still have a design that's better than what you've got.

### 7. Calendar embed — the conversion point

Repeat the CTA heading and the trust line, then the embed.

**Heading:** "Pick a time"
**Trust line:** "15 minutes. No pitch. Design is yours to keep."

**Embed:** Jordan's scheduling tool. **Confirm which tool** (Calendly, Cal.com, HubSpot, Google appointment schedule, etc.) and get the embed code or link.

**Qualifying questions on the booking form** — keep it to these, all short, mostly select/radio so they're fast on mobile:
1. Property name (text)
2. How do you take bookings today? (Airbnb only / Airbnb + Vrbo / A booking system like Hostaway, Guesty, Beds24, Lodgify / Something else)
3. Do you have a website? (No / Yes — enter URL)
4. How many units? (1–2 / 3–5 / 6–10 / 10+)
5. Rough annual bookings through Airbnb (Under $25k / $25–50k / $50–100k / $100k+ / Prefer not to say)

These are the questions Jordan needs answered before the call. Getting them on the form means the call starts informed and a no-show still tells us something. Don't add more — every field costs conversions.

If the scheduling tool can't collect these, capture them on a short pre-form step before the calendar. Two-step is acceptable; three is not.

### 8. FAQ — five questions, collapsed

Only include questions that remove a booking objection. Direction:

- **Do I need a website already?** No. Most people we talk to either don't have one or have one they're embarrassed by. Either is fine.
- **I only use Airbnb. Does this still make sense?** Yes — that's the most common situation. We'll talk through what it takes to add direct booking alongside Airbnb without double-booking.
- **Is the design really free?** Yes. You keep it. No card, no contract.
- **What if I want you to build it?** We'll tell you what that costs on the second call. It's a monthly service. No pressure.
- **How long until I see the design?** About a week after the first call.

### 9. Confirmation state — after booking

Don't neglect this. Show-rate on booked calls is a real number we're tracking.

- Confirm the time, in their time zone, with an add-to-calendar button.
- Say what happens next in two sentences.
- Ask them to have one thing ready: a link to their current site or Airbnb listing.
- Fire the conversion event here (see tracking), **not** on page load.

## Design constraints

- **Mobile first.** Meta traffic is overwhelmingly mobile. Design at 375px first, then scale up. The hero CTA must be visible without scrolling on a standard phone.
- **Sticky CTA on mobile** once the user scrolls past the hero. Small, non-intrusive, same copy as the primary button.
- **Fast.** One page, no heavy frameworks, compress the hero image aggressively. Target under 2 seconds on a mid-range phone on LTE.
- **Match the ad visually.** Dark charcoal base, orange/rust CTA, glamping-dome imagery, the small-caps eyebrow treatment. Continuity from ad to page is a conversion factor.
- **No navigation.** No header links, no footer links except legal (privacy policy) and a plain "Captive Demand" mark. No exits.
- **One CTA, repeated.** Hero, after section 3, after section 6, and the calendar itself. All say the same thing and all scroll/jump to the calendar.

## Tracking

- **Meta Pixel** on the page. Fire the `Lead` (or `Schedule`) standard event on the booking confirmation state only. Page views are not conversions.
- **GA4 via GTM.** Same event on confirmation. Track scroll depth and CTA clicks as secondary events so we can see where people drop.
- **UTM passthrough.** Capture UTMs and the Meta click ID (`fbclid`) and pass them into hidden fields on the booking form so we can tie booked calls back to specific ads. This matters — we're tracking cost per booked call per ad creative.
- Confirm Pixel ID, GA4 property, and GTM container with Jordan.

## What success looks like

The number that matters is **cost per booked call** by ad creative, and secondarily **show rate**. We don't have a target yet; the first job is to instrument well enough that we can set one after the first two weeks.

Things that would tell us the page isn't working:
- High click-through from the ad but low scroll past the hero → hero isn't matching the ad or isn't making the offer clear.
- Good scroll depth but low calendar starts → the "what's in it for me" or "what's the catch" sections aren't landing, or the ask feels too big.
- Calendar starts but low completions → too many fields, or the scheduling tool is clunky on mobile.
- Bookings but low show rate → confirmation state isn't doing its job, or we're attracting people who aren't serious. Reconsider the qualifying questions.

Instrument for all four.
